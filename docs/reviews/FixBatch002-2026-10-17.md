## R1 fixed — validate direct picks before board/history mutation

Location: [MultiplayerGameplayReducer.kt](../../app/src/main/java/com/bingo/multiplayer/domain/network/MultiplayerGameplayReducer.kt), called by `RootNavGraph.kt`'s `PICK_NUMBER` branch.

The immutable production reducer validates active match identity, participant, originating sender, open turn, expected picker, number range, duplicate number/turn and frozen/finished status before marking any board. A rejected decision returns the exact input state.

Peer history is diagnostic: it cannot add earlier closed-turn selections or future moves alongside the direct pick. Only the validated current move is added. The app requests an authoritative host snapshot for recovery instead of merging unvalidated history. Hosts send their accepted snapshot promptly after accepting a remote pick or rejecting a candidate. Existing snapshot sequencing and accepted-history fences from batch 001 are retained.

A valid move uses the original picked turn in all board marks, advances the turn once and gives the next turn its normal 30 seconds. Result calculation continues to use the existing winner/tie policies.

## R2 fixed — distinct sender/skipped-player identity and idempotent timeout

Locations: `RoomMessagePacket` in `OnlineRoomSyncManager.kt`, `FastPacketCodec.kt`, the production reducer and `RootNavGraph.kt`'s timeout producer/receiver.

Gameplay packets now carry `senderPlayerId` separately from `playerId`, plus `closedTurnNumber`. The eliminated-player skip factory captures the skipped player and closed turn before the UI changes to the next player. Codec encoding retains these fields; existing unversioned formats still decode.

The receiver accepts only the next transition from the currently open turn, with the expected skipped player and valid next player. Equal, obsolete, duplicate and future-gap timeouts do not reset the timer, increment counters or alter the board. Current online controller/self-pass behavior and existing Nearby peer timeout behavior are retained. Presence processing uses the actual sender, rather than treating the skipped target as the packet's source.

A valid host timeout also advances the guest's accepted turn fence and saves it with the resulting turn. After process restoration, replaying that timeout or an older host snapshot cannot roll the turn back or advance it twice.

## R3 fixed — reject results and surrender from another match

Locations: the shared gameplay identity guard and production `result`/`surrender` reducers, used before the corresponding Root callbacks mutate state.

Win, `GAME_OVER` and surrender messages must belong to the active match and known participants. Validation runs before gameplay presence processing. Explicit surrender can only originate from the surrendering participant. Explicit surrender receipts are tracked separately from missed turns and persist through restart/passive snapshots. Three missed turns are not treated as surrender; an actual surrender can still be accepted. Duplicate explicit surrender messages do not add misses repeatedly. Outgoing controls include the match identity when constructed, and delayed resends preserve the original identity.

A winning claim also requires an eligible known winner and actual Bingo on the accepted/reconciled board. If its final pick was missed, that pick must pass the same current-turn validation before a candidate board is prepared. Rejection leaves the original board untouched. A host snapshot handles missing earlier history. A finished host can publish its full terminal snapshot after the active recovery record has been cleared, supporting final-state catch-up without weakening active-match checkpoint ordering.

The online 1v1 legacy-message filter now also blocks `GAME_OVER`, leaving the normal V2 result path authoritative. Legacy explicit surrender and legitimate Bingo remain supported on the shared 3+ / Nearby path under the new validation.

## Follow-up review: three open P1 findings

All three paths were present in the pre-batch backup. They are separate from the repaired direct-packet validation paths. No fix for these findings was made in this batch.

### N1 — P1: surrender can leave a stale lifecycle turn and a player in future rotation

Locations: `RootNavGraph.kt`'s `SURRENDER` continuation around line 3348, `calculateNextTurnPlayerId` around line 1186, and `LifecycleAction.TurnPlayerInactive` / `TurnAutoSkipped` around lines 2235–2267.

Example: Stuart (A) surrenders during turn 2 in a 3-player match. The UI advances to Bob (B), turn 3. The surrender continuation does not call `multiplayerLifecyclePolicy.onTurnStarted`, so the policy still watches A/turn 2. After A has been silent for 10 seconds, the host can receive an inactivity choice for A; after another 10 seconds, the stale action can reset B's current turn and advance its number again.

Also, online 3+ rotation excludes `eliminatedPlayerIds`, while its continuation does not use the explicit departure receipts for rotation or update the lifecycle roster. A is not excluded from future assigned turns through that path, even though the new gameplay validator rejects picks after an explicit surrender. The screen and policy can therefore continue to disagree about departed participants.

Actual production policy plus source-flow reproduction:

```text
SURRENDER_POLICY policyTurn=2/A firstInactive=A autoSkipped=A screenAfterAction=4/B
```

Proposed fix for the next batch: commit explicit surrender into a consistent, persisted departure/rotation state. Update UI turn, lifecycle turn, frozen turn time and saved checkpoint together whenever surrender advances the turn. Ignore an inactivity action if it refers to a different accepted turn/player. Synchronize the departed roster to peers and preserve the existing explicit-surrender result policy and host ownership. Test surrender during an active turn, another player's turn, pause, restart and repeated delivery.

### N2 — P1: other delayed lifecycle packets still bypass match validation

Locations: the global presence block around `RootNavGraph.kt:2358`, the later `GAME_SYNC` seed guard around line 2787, and `LEAVE` / `HOST_LEFT` around line 3460.

Example A: the host is offline and a guest is correctly in HOLD. A delayed host `GAME_SYNC` from an earlier match arrives. The callback calls `onHostHeartbeat` before the `GAME_SYNC` branch rejects the wrong seed. That can start the 3-second ready countdown and unfreeze the game even though the current host has not returned.

Example B: a room starts a rematch with seed 42. A delayed `HOST_LEFT` from seed 41 enters the separate leave branch, which does not validate the active match identity. In an online 3+ match it can end the rematch and clear its recovery record. This is distinct from the fixed win/surrender branches.

Actual production policy plus explicit callback control-flow traces:

```text
STALE_SYNC_PRESENCE before=HOLD payloadRejected=true heartbeatAction=HostReturningCountdown after3s=HostResumed state=NORMAL
STALE_HOST_LEFT packetSeed=41 activeSeed=42 endsCurrentMatch=true
```

Proposed fix for the next batch: validate current match/room/connection and packet freshness before lifecycle events refresh match health or mutate match state. Carry the original match identity on explicit leave controls. Keep generic broker last-will/room-presence messages separate from a verified explicit departure in the current match; they must not be mistaken for a current-match result. Preserve intended host-exit and lobby policies. Test stale syncs and host-left messages across rematches, HOLD, reconnect generations and lobby transitions.

### N3 — P1: a pending local win can remain final after host rejection

Locations: `RootNavGraph.kt`'s local `executePick` outcome handling around lines 1998–2022, and guest `GAME_SYNC` outcome handling around line 3030.

Example: Stuart selects 20 near the end of turn 20 and his board completes Bingo. His phone immediately sets game-over, records the result and clears its recovery record. Kevin's controller has already expired turn 20 and rejects the delayed pick. The fresh host snapshot correctly removes 20 from Stuart's board, but outcome handling is guarded by `if (!isGameOver)`. Stuart can remain on a winning result despite his corrected board no longer having Bingo, while Kevin continues the match.

Real board engine and snapshot reconciler, with the local result flag traced from the callback:

```text
PENDING_WIN_ROLLBACK picker=A winningNumber=20 beforeBingo=false pendingBingo=true afterRejectionBingo=false localGameOver=true
```

This is the local optimistic result path, separate from the repaired incoming win-message checks. Online 1v1 uses its separate V2 coordinator; this finding concerns the legacy 3+ path.

Proposed fix for the next batch: keep immediate pick feedback, but distinguish a pending local Bingo from an accepted terminal match result. Finalize/record/clear recovery only after the original host confirms the winning pick/result. A rejection must remove pending presentation without leaving a recorded victory or final UI. Preserve existing winner/tie rules and add host-timeout-versus-winning-pick race tests through production code. No new referee or controller transfer is needed.

The follow-up probes use real lifecycle, board and snapshot functions plus clearly identified source-flow traces. They do not launch the Compose UI; these findings are not claimed as emulator reproductions. Source comparison with the pre-batch backup confirmed the same missing lifecycle-turn update and presence-before-seed-check ordering existed before this batch.
